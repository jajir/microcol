## 1. Summary

1. A player needs to see native-claimed land and pay for a tile before using it without the unpaid-land alarm.
2. The 2026-10-17 source catalogue at `8f3ce118` reports no native claim or purchase state; no runtime result is claimed.
3. This requirement owns claim radius, land purchase and the unpaid-use event fed to R-0153. It does not own the entire relation model or native trading market.

## 2. Behavior

1. Camps and villages claim a one-square radius; cities claim two. The map/confirmation makes the settlement's claim and whether this European has paid visible before claiming, improving or working land.
2. Buying one claimed tile costs `ceil(100 × k)` gold, doubled for a capital's claim, where `k` is the accepted difficulty factor. Successful payment is atomic and permanent for that European and tile. Insufficient gold, cancel, stale ownership/quote or invalid tile changes neither money nor rights.
3. Taking a newly unpaid claimed tile adds the accepted one-time +5 local alarm. Each unpaid worked or claimed tile adds +2 per owner update through R-0153; paid use does not. The same tile is not charged twice for the same cause in one update. Save/Continue retains the payment and does not repeat a one-time taking event.
4. Before recruiting Minuit, a claimed tile requires the ordinary payment to avoid unpaid-use alarm. After recruitment, future land payments are waived and peaceful use no longer causes unpaid-land alarm; previous payments are not refunded. Other native anger causes still apply. UC-060/098 compare just inside/outside camp/village and city radii, unpaid versus paid work/improvement, capital price and before/after Minuit.

5. Agreed 2026-10-20 ([shared rule](../gap-analysis.md#r-0152-refinement)): Give each claimed tile one owning settlement: nearest settlement, then stable settlement ID. That owner receives purchase money and local effects. One purchase permanently satisfies that European player’s unpaid-use obligation on the tile, even if a later claim-owner change occurs; do not charge a hidden second claim.
