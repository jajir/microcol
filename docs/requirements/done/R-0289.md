## 1. Summary

1. Players should recognize small rolling sea waves rather than the thick, peaked marks resembling hills.
2. Source inspection at `0530127059ce92e6e855410e288d0fd2290c1475` shows a single drawing appearing on 5% of eligible water squares, with placement reconsidered every four seconds; the wave itself has no animation frames.
3. Apply the selected low rolling shape to ocean and High Seas in four frames. High Seas marks stay present on every eligible square. Preserve lake identity, other terrain frequencies, meaningful overlays, fog and gameplay.

## 2. Behavior

1. Opening or continuing a game shows thin, shallow waves with a small rounded rolling lip on explored ocean and High Seas. There is no new control or confirmation; leaving the map follows the existing navigation.
2. Visible waves cycle through four slightly changing shapes, with different timing across locations. High Seas always retains a wave; a stationary preview or stopped animation still displays a complete wave.
3. Ocean keeps its 5% placement probability and staggered changes. Lake decoration is unchanged. Existing resource and terrain attributes retain priority, and unknown squares disclose no wave or terrain identity.
4. Panning, reloading and Continue preserve deterministic frame offsets for the saved map. Static help uses the first wave frame, matching each terrain's colour; High Seas retains its existing light colour and travel marker. Movement, production and saved-game data do not change.
