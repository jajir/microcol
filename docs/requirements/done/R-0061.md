## 1. Summary

1. The player needs expert tool production to conserve raw material while retaining the expert’s higher capacity.

2. Migration observation (2026-10-14): R-0023 supplies a purchasable master blacksmith. Expertise multiplies full output without multiplying full input; the accepted purchase journey still observes 3 ore producing 6 tools.

3. Scope: the outcome and examples below. Selected and refined on 2026-10-16; it does not reopen the delivered origin scope or close a whole UC.

## 2. Behavior

Agreed acceptance, refined on 2026-10-16; inherited resolved decisions remain agreed.

1. At the agreed smith-house tier, an expert with six ore can produce six tools and consumes six ore; three ore yields three tools and zero ore yields none.

2. An ordinary worker retains the agreed three-ore → three-tool baseline with sufficient input.

3. Multiple workers share the actual ore stock; their combined consumption never exceeds it and no ore is consumed twice.

4. Preview, next-turn stocks and save/continue agree. Buying and transporting the expert remains the completed R-0023 behavior.

5. Accepted behavior (2026-10-15; [economy, production and education](../../gap-analysis.md#economy-production-and-education)): Preserve the ordinary smith-house expert example of 6 ore consumed for 6 tools; use the shared input-limited production order.

Ordinary smith-house 3 ore → 3 tools remains unchanged; expert 6/3/0 available ore → 6/3/0 tools. Do not introduce a separate expert production pipeline.

2. Reuse that model-owned potential/input calculation in `ColonyJobChoice` so the R-0056 job menu shows six required ore for six potential expert tools and warns when only three are available. Shared colony statistics, actual update and saved continuation must agree. Verify two workers sharing one building's available ore cannot spend or produce it twice. Preserve existing building ratios and unrelated production chains; do not implement the wider specialist roster or building-tier backlog.
