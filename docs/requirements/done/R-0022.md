## 2. Behavior

1. Choose the actual passenger even when only one is eligible. Adjacent map transfer costs one passenger point and no ship points; same-port/pier transfer costs zero, including at zero points. Landing on an own colony leaves the person outside its workforce. Cancel/stale selection preserves identities and cargo.

1. Resolved on 2026-10-13 using the [original manual](../../Sid_Meiers_Colonization_manual.pdf), printed pages 25–26 (PDF pages 31–32): ordinary movement normally spends one move to enter a square, boarding moves from adjacent land onto the ship, and selected passengers move ashore. MicroCol policy for this slice: one passenger point for an adjacent map transfer, no ship points; reject a passenger with zero points. The manual does not state a numerical cost for a transfer within a port. Such a same-port/pier transfer costs zero and preserves points, including zero. This replaces the old unconditional exhaustion on boarding/shore landing. Terrain-dependent movement and Atlantic crossing duration remain their separate rules; Q-11 voyage timing does not block individual passenger transfer.

2. Resolved on 2026-10-13: land on the owned colony square outside the workforce. Printed page 48 (PDF page 54) distinguishes units in the colony square from working colonists; printed page 50 (PDF page 56) assigns work by explicitly placing a colonist in a building. This supports keeping landing separate from employment. A ship must be in that same accessible coastal port for a local dock transfer, or on a legal adjacent water square for map landing. The existing port predicate accepted any owned colony. For this dependency, use the manual's eight-direction map movement and coastal-settlement restriction (printed page 25): an owned colony is coastal when at least one valid adjacent square is water, including diagonals. An isolated water area qualifies; no ocean-connectivity algorithm is introduced. This precise adjacency policy is MicroCol's interpretation, not a numerical definition supplied by the manual. Test orthogonal/diagonal water and inland rejection. The manual also describes automatic boarding and automatic disembarkation on ship arrival (printed page 26); this requirement implements explicit individual transfers and does not claim to implement those automatic orders.
