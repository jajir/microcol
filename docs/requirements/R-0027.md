1. Status: Implemented and locally verified — full regression passed; publication pending

3. Related: P21; DIP-001; UC-068 (Hra), founding slice. See the [rule catalogue](../gap-analysis.md),

4. Dependencies: [R-0015](R-0015.md) rival roster; [R-0022](R-0022.md) landing; existing Z02 founding model.
5. Primary area: [A08](areas.md#a08).
6. Related areas: A05 legal movement and knowledge; A07 colony founding; A04 AI turn completion.

## 1. Summary

1. Give the first rival a bounded economic objective: land a colonist and establish a viable first colony instead of only wandering.

2. The initial catalogue audit found only movement, attacks and embarkation in `SimpleAiPlayer`, with no founding or economic planning in `turnStarted`. Chapter 3 records the implemented first-colony behavior.

3. One rival fleet→landing→first colony objective and a safe no-site outcome. Sustained production planning, trade, expansion and military strategy remain later DIP-001 slices.

## 2. Behavior

Implementation is authorized by the request to implement R-0019 onward. The
following decisions distinguish original-manual rules from this bounded AI policy.

1. Add a selectable **Rival expedition** free-play world with a French ship and
   an existing colonist aboard, but no French colonies. Preserve both R-0015
   worlds and their agreed Quebec/Montreal content. The new world uses the
   ordinary New Game, turn, save and Continue paths. From that reference start,
   the rival explores known/reachable options, lands its colonist and founds
   one colony at a model-valid site. A test-only starting fleet is insufficient.

2. Founding uses the same ownership, location, naming and identity rules as the human action. It neither creates bonus settlers nor bypasses movement/cargo restrictions.

3. First-colony policy: select an existing eligible founder by stable unit ID;
   prefer a reachable coastal settlement with an available land field whose
   model corn yield covers that founder's food consumption. Rank routes by
   movement distance, then available food yield, then fixed map coordinates.
   Read terrain and occupancy only after the location is known to that player.
   Explore reachable known frontier positions when no known candidate exists;
   revelation must come from ordinary unit movement. This ranking is MicroCol
   policy, not an algorithm specified by the original manual. A blocked path
   or taken site triggers bounded replanning rather than an endless turn.

4. If no legal site exists within the chosen search policy, end the turn safely and retry later. Existing human colonies and units remain untouched.

5. Save/load during travel reconstructs the goal deterministically from live
   ownership, unit positions and player knowledge. Stop this first-colony
   strategy once the rival owns a colony. Do not introduce persistent planning
   state unless implementation evidence shows reconstruction is insufficient.

6. The shared founding operation rejects invalid live state, ownership, terrain,
   name or identity before changing the world. Follow the manual's land and
   non-mountain founding rule for human and AI alike. The AI's coastal/food
   preference is stricter than general founding permission; it must not become
   an undocumented restriction on human founding. Keep the existing economy
   in scope: this requirement does not claim to implement the manual's missing
   town-commons production or general rival economic management.

## 4. Open questions

1. Resolved from the [original manual](../Sid_Meiers_Colonization_manual.pdf#page=44),
   printed pp. 38–39 (PDF pp. 44–45): founding is on land except mountains;
   site choice should support its population with food. For this first-colony
   AI, require a coastal site with an available land food field covering the
   founder's model consumption. The land-field preference avoids depending on
   later dock/economic planning; it is AI policy rather than a new universal
   founding rule. Use actual model yields, including terrain improvements.

2. Reference start decision: add the selectable expedition world while retaining
   R-0015's existing worlds. The implemented reference route and shared action
   costs establish the three-turn bound in chapter 3.8, checked through actual
   turn events and the ordinary UI. This is a bound for that reference world,
   not a promise that every possible map can be settled within three turns.

3. The [manual's European powers discussion](../Sid_Meiers_Colonization_manual.pdf#page=85),
   printed p. 79, supports colonizing rivals but prescribes no deterministic
   search ranking or turn count. Those details above are explicit MicroCol
   choices. Continue with implementation; no new product approval is required.
