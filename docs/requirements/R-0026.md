# R-0026 — Persist European relations independently of player names

1. Status: Implemented; final CI acceptance and publication pending
2. Updated: 2026-10-14
3. Related: P20; DIP-002/003; UC-069 (Hra), relation-state slice. See the [rule catalogue](../gap-analysis.md#european-diplomacy-and-rivalry), [use cases](../use-cases.md) and [work packages](../implementation-plan.md#seznam-pracovnich-baliku).
4. Dependencies: [R-0015](R-0015.md) participant identity; [R-0028](R-0028.md) owns the atomic treaty-breaking attack path.
5. Primary area: [A08](areas.md#a08).
6. Related areas: A09 legal attacks; A04 persistence; A03 relation display.

## 1. Summary

1. Introduce a shared bilateral peace/war state with persistence, a basic political readout and an actual effect on routine AI targeting.

2. Source baseline `61789f81`: `Player` already has a final internal participant key (`name`), separate from mutable `displayName`. `PlayerStore` rejects duplicate keys. `ModelPo` has no diplomatic pairs; `UnitStorage.getEnemyUnits*` means all nonowners, including callers that enforce movement and cargo occupancy. `SimpleUnitBehavior` and the simple AI's movement planning lack a treaty gate. A normal move can also capture an unguarded foreign colony.

3. Scope is European colonizing players and their bilateral state. Keep Crown/rebellion behavior separate. Negotiation dialogue, tribute, alliances, privateers, native attitudes and detailed foreign intelligence remain distinct catalogue gaps. This slice does not claim the whole UC-069 journey or actual attack integration before R-0028.

## 2. Behavior

Implementation is authorized by the active R-0019-and-later goal. The decisions
below resolve this slice against chapter 4; they are not verification evidence.

1. Each unordered pair of distinct European colonizing players has exactly one `PEACE` or `WAR` state. Use the existing immutable participant keys, never editable display names. Crown players are excluded by their model role, not by the spelling of their names. Both free-play reference maps explicitly start their Dutch/French pair at peace. Other scenarios declare their own relations; a deliberately hostile fixture must say so.

2. A basic European-relations report may show all colonizing participants' names and bilateral peace/war states from the start, including third-party pairs. The manual permits these broad political facts before detailed intelligence is available. Do not expose hidden positions, colony counts, population, force strength or goods through this report. Display-name changes affect labels only; identities, ownership and every relation remain intact.

3. Model operations declare hostilities from peace or record an accepted peace from war for one pair only. Validate a running game (including rejection after `stop()` or a game-over result), the acting current player, registered participant identity, distinct colonizing endpoints and the expected prior state before mutation. Reject unknown, self, Crown, wrong-turn, duplicate or stale transitions without changing any pair, unit, movement, stock or treasury. A peace operation means a treaty already accepted by the parties; it is not a unilateral peace offer or an AI negotiation algorithm.

4. Provide a localized, keyboard-accessible report through the existing Statistics screen. For the current human player's peaceful rival, offer an explicit declaration review naming both sides and stating that it starts war. Selecting a rival or opening the report changes nothing. Confirm applies the transition once; Cancel/Escape changes nothing. Bind the review to the original game, actor, target and state; replacement of the game or a changed relation rejects and refreshes instead of applying an old choice. A standalone declaration is the MicroCol entry point for this slice, not a claim about an original-game menu command. Do not expose a unilateral “make peace” button while negotiation is absent.

5. Routine AI attack selection, movement toward an immediate attack and entry into an unguarded foreign colony respect peace between colonizing players. Shared attack and movement queries distinguish hostility from foreign occupancy: peaceful foreign units still block movement, stacking and passenger destinations exactly as before. Until R-0028 adds atomic treaty-breaking attack confirmation, an ordinary attack requires existing hostility; the explicit R-0026 declaration is the available entry point. A hostile defender inside a third party’s peaceful colony does not permit a capture through combat. Peace must not enable silent colony capture. Existing Crown behavior remains governed by the rebellion path. Human attack confirmation and atomic war-plus-combat execution belong to R-0028 and remain an explicit dependency until verified there.

6. A successful capture keeps the capturing force on the colony tile with its equipment and identity. It transfers the colony and inhabitants without automatically assigning the force to productive work or returning its equipment to storage. The AI's adjacent unguarded-colony entry uses the ordinary model movement operation, including its movement cost and live checks. This removes a discovered failure where automatic field assignment attempted to unequip an armed capturer in a still-foreign colony. A port entry plans all hostile ships’ legal adjacent-sea retreats before spending movement or changing ownership. Neutral occupants or an unavailable retreat reject the entry without partial effects. This preserves the existing ship-escape path while making its rejection atomic; it is not a new naval-combat rule. Broader capture/defender-resolution rules remain outside this relation-state slice.

7. Save/load retains every pair and state without recomputing them from names or replaying transitions. Require a complete pair set (an explicit empty list when fewer than two colonizing players exist); reject missing, unknown, self, duplicate/reversed, Crown or invalid-state entries without changing the source save. Renaming a participant or reordering stored players/pair endpoints cannot create another relation. Reading a save or reopening the report never declares war or peace. Preserve a reached game-over result on disk so loading cannot re-enable transitions; discarding a model instance is not itself a saved game-over result.

## 3. Implementation steps

1. Keep bilateral state and its transitions in `model`, with pair data in `model.store` and serialization through `ModelPo`/`Model`. Reuse `Player`/`PlayerStore` keys and role checks; no UUID migration, GUI dependency or parallel player registry. Keep one authoritative unordered-pair record so forward and reverse queries cannot disagree. Update bundled maps, test fixtures and programmatic model builders directly to the required schema; backward compatibility is not required.

2. Inspect all `getEnemyUnits*`, attack and capture callers. Preserve foreign occupancy behavior; add/use a model hostility query for routine AI decisions. Cover `SimpleAiPlayer`'s immediate-target planning, `SimpleUnitBehavior` attack/capture paths and normal movement into an unguarded colony. Do not fix only the visible attack call and leave a capture bypass. Retain the existing Crown path and verify its tests.

3. Reuse the existing Statistics navigation and modal infrastructure for the small report/declaration review, with English/Czech text, explicit confirmation and cancellation, and readable outcome or stale-choice feedback. Add no economic/military intelligence or negotiation simulator. Keep the model authoritative; a report is a read operation. Update the package responsibility map only where responsibilities actually change.

4. Add proposed `TC_33_F25_european_relations_test` with a human and two colonizing rivals. Cover the initial political readout without hidden intelligence, cancellation, one confirmed declaration, unchanged third-party pairs, renamed display labels and Continue; add a stale relation/replaced-game confirmation and a Czech keyboard path. Model cases cover symmetry, three-player isolation, identity/order stability, every invalid transition, accepted peace and malformed saves. AI cases prove no peacetime attack or empty-colony capture, war permits the existing hostile path, and an accepted peace stops that path again without weakening foreign occupancy. Tests of accepted peace prepare a mutually accepted treaty in the model; they do not substitute for a missing negotiation journey.

5. Apply the shared formatter, run targeted model/AI and named player scenarios plus source-contract checks, then full Docker regression. Record actual results and affected UC evidence. Commit/push and verify exact CI, Pages, manifest and downloaded archive through the [delivery workflow](../development-builds.md).

## 4. Open questions

1. Resolved on 2026-10-14: initial Dutch/French relations in the two reference maps are explicitly `PEACE`. The [original manual](../Sid_Meiers_Colonization_manual.pdf), printed page 81 (PDF 87), defines attacks as acts of war and says war persists until peace is obtained. It does not prescribe every custom scenario's initial relationship; the reference-map initialization is MicroCol policy. Combat fixtures may explicitly start at war; missing data is not inferred as peace.

2. Resolved on 2026-10-14: implement and test the accepted-peace transition in the model, without pretending that an offer is acceptance or providing unilateral peace UI. Printed page 82 (PDF 88) describes bilateral cessation of hostilities after a treaty and negotiations dependent on the rival's response. Negotiation, tribute and alliance journeys remain outside this bounded state slice. Keep their UC-069 gaps open.

3. Resolved on 2026-10-14: broad political relations can be visible from the start. The Foreign Affairs Advisor on printed page 79 (PDF 85) initially reports who is at war with whom; detailed population/colony/force comparisons come after Jan de Witt. This supports the basic relation readout without inventing contact flags or revealing detailed world information.

4. Resolved on 2026-10-14: use `Player.name` as the existing immutable internal key and `displayName` only as a label. The inspected constructor, save/load references and duplicate-key check already establish this separation; changing the display name must not rekey pairs. This is a MicroCol identity decision, not a claim about the historical save format.


5. Resolved on 2026-10-14: the [original manual](../Sid_Meiers_Colonization_manual.pdf), printed page 32 (PDF 38), distinguishes the forces entering a captured settlement from its inhabitants, who change allegiance and continue working. Printed page 46 (PDF 52) places soldier/dragoon roles on the map rather than in productive jobs. Keeping the capturer equipped on the colony tile is the implementation interpretation used here; no automatic workforce assignment is implied. Source inspection found that the previous capture path called field placement before changing colony ownership, which conflicts with authoritative equipment-return validation. This does not implement the separate civilian-defense combat described on page 32.
