# R-0022 — Embark and disembark an individual colonist safely

1. Status: In progress
2. Updated: 2026-10-13
3. Related: P08; CARGO-002/003/007/008; UC-024/UC-025 (Hra). See the [rule catalogue](../gap-analysis.md),
   [use cases](../use-cases.md) and [work packages](../implementation-plan.md#seznam-pracovnich-baliku).
4. Dependencies: Existing cargo model; the coastal-port predicate needed from [R-0004](R-0004.md). That requirement was still a draft at inspection, so this change must implement and verify this prerequisite.
5. Primary area: [A06](areas.md#a06).
6. Related areas: A05 legal locations/ownership; A07 colony admission; A03 selection.

## 1. Summary

1. Complete the manual passenger transfer needed to land an expedition and bring a purchased expert home.

2. Existing capability: TC22/TC_03 boards the same colonist from the European pier and returns them through real drag/drop; identity and equipment remain unchanged. Existing TC01 exercises two colonists between a colony, ship and shore. These paths must be preserved; selective landing, invalid/full/foreign targets and precise movement accounting remain separate acceptance gaps.

3. One ordinary or equipped colonist at a time between own ship and legal shore/port/pier. Treasure, artillery, automatic boarding and passenger priority queues remain separate.

## 2. Behavior

Implementation is authorized by the active R-0019-and-later goal. The decisions
below use the original manual and explicitly identify MicroCol policy. Existing
partial tests do not establish acceptance of the complete requirement.

1. An owned colonist can board an eligible owned ship at an allowed location when a cargo space is available; equipment travels with the person and uses the same passenger space. Validate the running game, current owner, live object identities, passenger eligibility, source place, carrier, slot ownership/capacity and destination before changing either place. Restore saved places through the persistence path, not a gameplay command.

2. Show legal destinations when disembarking. One selected passenger leaves; all other passengers and goods remain aboard. For a ship-to-shore gesture, choose the passenger before confirmation and allow cancellation even when only one passenger is eligible. Retain the existing dock drag/drop for an individual passenger. Arrival on an owned colony square leaves the person outside the workforce; employment is a separate action.

3. Full ship, foreign ship, distant shore, inland destination or invalid passenger location rejects without losing the unit or changing another cargo slot.

4. Cancel a destination choice without issuing a model command. Success keeps the selected person discoverable at the destination. An adjacent map boarding or landing spends one passenger action point and requires at least one; the carrier spends none. A transfer within the same owned coastal colony port or the European port/pier spends no movement and is allowed at zero points. Preserve remaining movement during those local transfers. The command rechecks eligibility after the choice; rejection shows an explanation and leaves all places, equipment, other cargo and points unchanged.

5. Save/load before and after each transfer preserves exactly one location for the colonist and consistent carrier references.

Reuse the project message-window/buttons and translated unit descriptions for a cancellable choice; use model eligibility for previews, lists and dock drag acceptance. ## 4. Open questions

1. Resolved on 2026-10-13 using the [original manual](../Sid_Meiers_Colonization_manual.pdf), printed pages 25–26 (PDF pages 31–32): ordinary movement normally spends one move to enter a square, boarding moves from adjacent land onto the ship, and selected passengers move ashore. MicroCol policy for this slice: one passenger point for an adjacent map transfer, no ship points; reject a passenger with zero points. The manual does not state a numerical cost for a transfer within a port. Such a same-port/pier transfer costs zero and preserves points, including zero. This replaces the old unconditional exhaustion on boarding/shore landing. Terrain-dependent movement and Atlantic crossing duration remain their separate rules; Q-11 voyage timing does not block individual passenger transfer.

2. Resolved on 2026-10-13: land on the owned colony square outside the workforce. Printed page 48 (PDF page 54) distinguishes units in the colony square from working colonists; printed page 50 (PDF page 56) assigns work by explicitly placing a colonist in a building. This supports keeping landing separate from employment. A ship must be in that same accessible coastal port for a local dock transfer, or on a legal adjacent water square for map landing. The existing port predicate accepted any owned colony. For this dependency, use the manual's eight-direction map movement and coastal-settlement restriction (printed page 25): an owned colony is coastal when at least one valid adjacent square is water, including diagonals. An isolated water area qualifies; no ocean-connectivity algorithm is introduced. This precise adjacency policy is MicroCol's interpretation, not a numerical definition supplied by the manual. Test orthogonal/diagonal water and inland rejection. The manual also describes automatic boarding and automatic disembarkation on ship arrival (printed page 26); this requirement implements explicit individual transfers and does not claim to implement those automatic orders.
